Biography & Background

Bridging Historical Inquiry with Frontline Reporting

Md. Mirazul Islam is a dedicated journalist and researcher based in Khulna, Bangladesh. He holds a Bachelor of Arts (Honours) in History and Civilization from Khulna University (2025), where he cultivated a disciplined approach to archival inquiry, oral history, and critical content analysis.

Alongside his academic research, Mirazul has built an extensive track record in journalism across national and regional media outlets. He currently serves as Khulna University Correspondent for Daily Kaler Kantho, Contributor for Daily Observer, and Contributor for Bangladesh Guardian. From 2022 to 2026, he served in key executive leadership positions at the Khulna University Journalists' Association (KUJA), advancing from Associate Member to General Secretary and Executive Member.

Academic Thesis / Working Paper 2024 - 2025

Post-Colonial State Formation and Political Violence in Bengal: An Archival Content Analysis

International History & Political Science

Investigating how partition, colonial border demarcations, and state authority shaped patterns of political mobilization and grassroots conflict in post-1947 Bengal. The study examines historical records, colonial commission gazettes, and post-liberation archives to understand systemic drivers of collective violence.

Methodology: Archival Research & Qualitative Historical Analysis
Institution: History and Civilization Discipline, Khulna University
Focus Area: Khulna & Greater Bengal Borderlands

Field Survey & Policy Monograph 2025 - Present

Ecological Vulnerability, Salinity Influx, and Climate Migration in Southwest Bangladesh

Climate Justice & Environmental Displacement

An empirical assessment mapping the intersection of freshwater depletion, cyclone vulnerability, and demographic displacement across riverbank communities. Combines geo-spatial water salinity readings with in-depth qualitative oral histories from displaced women and smallholder farmers.

Methodology: Empirical Field Survey & Mixed-Method Interviews (n=120 households)
Institution: Independent Research / Khulna University Research Society (KURS)
Focus Area: Sundarbans Coastal Belt (Khulna, Bagerhat, Satkhira)

Conference Paper & UNESCO Synthesis 2025

Digital Surveillance, Media Censorship, and Physical Safety Risks for Grassroots Journalists in South Asia

Media Studies & Human Rights

Examines the escalating risks faced by investigative and campus journalists operating outside metropolitan news hubs. Evaluates digital tracking, phishing, legal intimidations, and physical security threats, proposing decentralized peer-protection protocols and verifiable encryption standards.

Methodology: Comparative Legal Review & Semi-Structured Focus Groups
Institution: UNESCO & News Network Media Safety Framework
Focus Area: Regional & Campus Newsrooms, Bangladesh

Student Mobilization, Autonomous Campus Press, and Democratic Transition in Bangladesh

Social Movements & Higher Education
